## English Unit 5 + 6: Modal verbs + Conditionals
Modal verbs: obligation, prohibition, advice
 Meaning Present Past 
 Obligation (Pflicht) must (my rule) / have to (external rule) had to 
 No obligation don't have to / needn't didn't have to / didn't need to 
 Prohibition (Verbot) mustn't / can't wasn't allowed to / couldn't 
 Advice (Rat) should / ought to should have + past participle (Kritik: You should have studied.) 
 Falle: mustn't = darf nicht. don't have to = muss nicht.
 Modal verbs: speculation + deduction
 Sicherheit Present / future Past 
 sicher ja must be must have been 
 vielleicht may / might / could be may / might / could have been 
 sicher nein can't be can't / couldn't have been 
 His lights are off – he must be asleep. · She can't have seen us, she didn't wave. · They might have missed the bus. 
 Conditionals
 Type If-part Main part Use 
 Zero present simple present simple always true: If you heat ice, it melts. 
 First present simple will + verb real future: If it rains, I will stay home. 
 Second past simple would + verb unreal now: If I had money, I would buy it. 
 Third past perfect (had + 3rd form) would have + 3rd form unreal past: If I had studied, I would have passed. 
 Never will/would in the if-part! «If I were you…» (2nd) = advice.

## Begriffe
- must vs have to: must = my own rule · have to = external rule
- mustn't vs don't have to: mustn't = verboten · don't have to = nicht nötig
- Speculation past: 3 levels: must have (sure) · might/could have (maybe) · can't have (sure not)
- 1st conditional: If + present, will + verb
- 2nd conditional: If + past, would + verb
- 3rd conditional: If + had + 3rd form, would have + 3rd form
- Money phrasal verbs: save up, pay back, put aside, splash out, get by, run out of, take out (a loan), come into
- Health idioms: under the weather, fit as a fiddle, on the mend, a frog in my throat

## Übungsfragen mit Lösung
- «You ___ smoke here. It's forbidden.» → mustn't (mustn't = prohibition.)
- «It's Sunday, you ___ get up early.» (no obligation) → don't have to (don't have to = not necessary.)
- Past of «I have to work»: → I had to work (must has no past → had to.)
- «You failed. You ___ more.» (criticism) → should have studied (should have + 3rd form = criticism of the past.)
- «The lights are off. They ___ be asleep.» (sure) → must (must = I'm sure it's true.)
- «She ___ be at home – I just saw her in town.» (sure not) → can't (can't = I'm sure it's not true.)
- «He didn't answer. He ___ have heard the phone.» (maybe) → might not (might (not) have = maybe in the past.)
- «The road is wet. It ___ rained.» (sure, past) → must have (must have + 3rd form = sure about the past.)
- Zero conditional: «If you heat water to 100 °C, it ___.» → boils (Always true → present + present.)
- First conditional: «If it rains tomorrow, we ___ at home.» → will stay (Real future → if + present, will.)
- Second conditional: «If I ___ rich, I would travel.» → were (Unreal present → past simple (were).)
- Third conditional – when do you use it? → For an imagined PAST that didn't happen (regret, criticism) (If + past perfect (had + V3), would have + V3.)
- «If she ___ the bus, she wouldn't have been late.» → hadn't missed
- «If they had trained harder, they ___ the match.» → would have won
- Which sentence is a correct third conditional? → If I had known, I would have helped you. (Never «would have» in the if-part!)
- Third conditional: «If I had studied, I ___ passed.» → would have (Unreal past → would have + 3rd form.)
- Which is correct? → If I had known, I would have come. (3rd conditional.)
- «If I were you, I would…» is used for… → giving advice (2nd conditional for advice.)
- «save up» means… → sparen (save up for a car.)
- «pay back» means… → zurückzahlen (pay back a loan.)
- «splash out» means… → viel Geld für etwas Schönes ausgeben (splash out on a holiday.)
- «get by» means… → mit wenig Geld über die Runden kommen (We get by on 2000 a month.)
- «run out of money» means… → kein Geld mehr haben (run out of = ausgehen.)
- «under the weather» means… → sich leicht krank fühlen (I'm a bit under the weather.)
- «fit as a fiddle» means… → kerngesund (Idiom: very healthy.)
- «cost an arm and a leg» means… → sehr teuer sein (That car cost an arm and a leg.)
- «on the mend» means… → auf dem Weg der Besserung (He's on the mend after the flu.)
- «pull someone's leg» means… → jemanden veräppeln (Are you pulling my leg?)
